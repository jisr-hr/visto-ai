---
title: Running the service
description: Run one instance per database.
---

- Run one instance per database. A second one stops at once with `another_instance_running`, and the first keeps running. It loads no extension module.
- An extension module that cannot be loaded stops the start: the log has `extension_failed` with the module's name and a code, then `start_failed`, and the process exits with 1 before it listens (see [docs/guide/extension-contract.md](extension-contract.md)).
- At each start, after the lock and the migrations and before anything reads a setting, the service syncs the editable part of the config with the database (see [Configuration](configuration.md)) and logs one `settings` line with `from_file` and `from_ui`. A config that fails alone is refused as before, with its `config:` lines and exit code 2, and nothing is copied. A config that passes alone but not with the settings saved in the UI put back stops the start: each issue is a `config:` line, then, when some setting came from the UI, the sentence `Saved in the UI: ...; with them config.yml does not pass.` naming up to 10 of them, then `start_failed`, and the process exits with 1 before it listens. These lines are plain text written as the other lines of a refused start are, not JSON; nothing was copied, and the tables `settings` and `settings_file` stay as they were. Changing those settings in the config, to another value or removing them, lets it start.
- If the database restarts, the service notices that it has lost its lock, exits, and Compose starts it again.
- Connect to Postgres directly. The lock and the statement timeout do not work through a pooler in transaction mode.
- Stopping the service (`docker compose stop`) waits up to 10 minutes for the reviews that are running. The Compose file allows 660 seconds for it. A second signal ends it at once.
- Every review costs money. The cost of a run is what the model endpoint reports with each answer; OpenRouter, the default, reports it in US dollars. For an endpoint that reports no cost, it is computed from `model.prices`, in whatever currency those prices are written in. With neither, the cost is recorded as 0.
- The daily limit, `limits.spendPerDay` (50 when not written; a change saved in Settings is read at the next look), is a number in the unit of those recorded costs. The service adds up the cost of the runs, and of the calls of the judge of lessons (see [Lessons](lessons-and-reverts.md#lessons)), recorded since the start of the UTC day, so it can only see what is recorded: with an endpoint that reports no cost and no `prices`, it never stops anything, and it raises the alert `cost_unknown`. At 80% of the limit it raises `spend_80`, and at the limit `spend_limit`. Reviews then stop starting until the next UTC day, and the judge makes no call; the reviews already running finish. These three alerts are raised once per run of the service, so a restart can raise them again; `spend_80` and `spend_limit` are also raised once per UTC day, and again when the limit is raised in Settings above what is spent and the spend later reaches 80% or the limit again. At most `limits.queueConcurrency` reviews run at once (2 when not written; read at the start, so a change saved in Settings waits for the next one).
- The jobs of the merge gate (writing the check, `/done`, a skip, a re-run) run in a second lane of 4 places. It is not paused with the reviews, by the daily limit or by the model's key, and it waits for no review. There are never two review jobs for one pull request at once, and never two gate jobs for one pull request at once; one gate job may run beside a review job of the same pull request. The withdrawals of the app's approvals run in a third lane of 2 places, which is not paused either (see [Automatic approval](automatic-approval.md)). Stopping the service waits for the three lanes.
- When the model's key is refused or its account is out of credit, reviews pause for 5 minutes at a time and the service raises one alert, `model_error`, and `model_ok` when the key works again. The jobs wait, but not for ever: a job that has waited 24 hours is dropped as `too_old`. A review refused after it had already made model calls uses up one of the job's three starts. Alerts go to the log always, to `alerts.webhook` when you wrote it, and, out of dry run, to the extension modules that have a sink. The alerts are `model_error`, `model_ok`, `spend_80`, `spend_limit`, `cost_unknown`, `redelivery_failed`, `feature_off`, `gate_check_failed`, `gate_bypass`, `extension_error`, `extension_ok`, `mcp_error`, `mcp_ok`, `memory_error`, `memory_ok`, `learning_stale`, `lessons_stale`, `dismissal_failed` and `stale_approval_merged`.
- An MCP server is first asked at the first review that opens it, not at start (see [MCP tools](mcp-tools.md)).
- A job ends without a review when its three starts are used up (`attempts_exhausted`), when it is a day old (`too_old`), when no installation is known for the pull request (`no_installation`), when another job for the pull request was already waiting as it was put back (`superseded`), or when the pull request is, by the time the job runs, not one to review: gone (`not_found`), closed, a draft, by an ignored author (`ignored_author`), against a base branch outside the filter (`base_filtered`), gated and reviewed only when asked (`gated`; see [The merge gate](merge-gate.md)), given a new head (`stale_head`; the new head gets a job of its own), or already reviewed at that head and base (`already_reviewed`; a `/review` is not ended this way, except in dry run). Out of dry run, `nothing_new` ends a push's job, after the engine has run without a model call, when the review would tell a person what the newest published one already tells (see Reviews after the first), unless automatic approval is on and that newest one was posted as an approval. A review that fails is recorded and its job ends `failed`; nothing is posted for it. Out of dry run there are more. Before anything is paid: `already_reviewed` when GitHub lists a review of the app's own on the live head that the database does not know of. After the review was computed and recorded: `head_moved` when the head or the base changed meanwhile (the new head gets a job; a command's own job starts over once, see `/review`), `closed` when the pull request was closed meanwhile, `publish_rejected` when GitHub refused the review even without its inline comments, and `render_failed` when its text could not be written.
- When GitHub cannot be reached, or refuses a read (reading the threads is one), before any model call, the run is recorded as failed and the job is tried again a minute later, and then 15 minutes after that: three starts in all. A pull request whose threads cannot be read (GitHub refuses the query, or the list is too long to read) gets no later review: each of its jobs ends `attempts_exhausted`. The list of the pull request's reviews, which the service reads to find its own, has a cap too (10 pages of 100); a pull request past it gets no review after that, in the same way.
- Out of dry run, a review that could not be posted for any other reason (GitHub could not be reached, or answered with an error) stays recorded and its job is tried again 15 minutes later, whatever its starts and whatever the error: GitHub may have created the review although it answered with an error, and the later start, which asks GitHub for the app's own reviews first, then adopts it rather than posting it again. A start that comes sooner (after a `/review`, a reopening or a restart) and finds no review of the app's own for that commit does not post the recorded review: GitHub may hold a review it does not list yet. The job waits until 15 minutes have passed since the review was recorded, or since the last later start that set out to post it (each such start writes its time on the recorded review first, and posts nothing when it cannot). A start that waits is not counted, and a `/review` that waits this way is told nothing meanwhile. So each real failure delays the review by up to 15 minutes. A review GitHub created and still does not list by then is posted a second time; this was not seen. The later start posts the recorded review without computing it again, while it is less than a day old.
- A restart of the database, like any restart of the service in the middle of a review, cuts the reviews that are running. They run again afterwards while they have starts left, and their model calls are paid for again. Out of dry run, a review already recorded when the restart came is posted afterwards, once 15 minutes have passed since it was recorded or last tried, without being computed again, provided its job has a start left and is less than a day old.

Setup notes:

- A database password that contains any of `@ : / # ?` must be percent-encoded in the database URL.
- If you change `server.port` in the config, change the port mapping and the health check in the Compose file too.

What to know before you expose it:

- The service holds a request's body in memory before it can check the signature: at most 25 MB for each of at most 20 requests at once. That is about 500 MB while bodies arrive, and about twice that at the moment a body is joined into one piece.
- A caller that sends slowly can hold one of the 20 places for up to about a minute. Twenty such callers, none of whom needs the secret, take every place, and real deliveries are answered `503 busy` meanwhile.
- An extension module's routes are public: the service checks nothing about who calls, and the module must check each request itself. Each module holds at most 10 requests of at most 1 MiB, beside what the webhook route holds. A caller that sends slowly holds one of a module's places for up to 30 seconds. An address under `/extensions/` that names no module with a route is answered `404` without being read.
- The Compose file publishes port 8080 on every interface of the host. TLS, and any limit on connections or body size per caller, belong to the proxy in front. Firewall the port, or publish it only where the proxy can reach it.
